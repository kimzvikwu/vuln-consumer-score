How the score works

Risk points = Σ (CVEs × severity points) + KEV additions × KEV points
Density = risk points ÷ (employees ÷ 1,000)
Score = 100 ÷ (1 + density ÷ cohort median density)

The median company in the current view scores 50. A company with half the median density scores 67; double the median scores 33. Grades: A ≥ 80, B ≥ 65, C ≥ 50, D ≥ 35, F below.

Attribution

A CVE counts against a company when its CVE record (the CNA's "affected" vendor, or CPE vendor added by CISA's ADP enrichment) names one of the company's brands. Where a company's own CNA published a record with no vendor named (Intel does this for all of its CVEs), the assigner is used. Acquisitions are rolled up: VMware, Brocade and Symantec into Broadcom; Splunk into Cisco; Juniper and Aruba into HPE; Red Hat and HashiCorp into IBM; Axis into Canon; CyberArk into Palo Alto Networks.

Severity

Highest CVSS base severity in the record (v4.0, v3.1, v3.0, then v2), from the CNA first and CISA's ADP enrichment second. Microsoft and others rate most findings "Important", which maps to CVSS High.

Read it with care

  • Disclosure is not insecurity. Companies with mature PSIRTs, bug bounties and their own CNA publish more CVEs. A low CVE count can mean fewer bugs, or fewer that were disclosed. The KEV weight exists because exploitation in the wild is the better signal of consumer harm.
  • Cloud services rarely get CVEs. SaaS-heavy companies (Salesforce, ServiceNow, Shopify, Meta, Amazon's retail side) fix issues server-side, so they look clean here by construction.
  • Headcount favours large conglomerates. Siemens, Hitachi and Samsung spread their CVEs across hundreds of thousands of employees, most of whom don't write software. Small product companies with large device fleets (D-Link, Netgear, Ubiquiti) take the opposite hit.
  • Employee counts are approximate, from the latest annual report available as of mid-2026, and the fiscal year is shown on hover. Correct them in the table.
  • 2026 volume roughly doubled across the whole CVE program (), largely from AI-assisted discovery. Compare companies within the same window rather than across years.
  • IBM includes Red Hat. Red Hat's CNA records many upstream open-source flaws (shipped in RHEL) as Red Hat-affected, so most of IBM's count is open-source components rather than IBM-authored code.
